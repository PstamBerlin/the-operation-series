01The Plastic

Shoes live or die on the filament. Regular hard plastic (PLA) makes a shoe-shaped brick. You want TPU — flexible filament — and the number on the spool (Shore hardness, like 85A or 95A) decides everything.

"The harder the TPU, the worse the shoe." Backed by the filament makers themselves: a whole shoe in hard 95A is "a heavy, dense shoe with zero shock absorption — it bends but doesn't bounce." (Siraya Tech)
60A · jelly75A85A90A95A+ · brick
~75A cloud mode

Ultra-soft, squishy like a running-shoe sole. Raw 75A filament is nearly unprintable (it buckles like a wet noodle) — this softness is what MorPhlex reaches after printing.

85A the sweet spot

Midsoles & insoles. Squishes under your heel, springs back with every step. Needs direct drive + slow printing (~15–30 mm/s), but this is the comfort zone.

90A firm flex

The easy printer: feeds cleanly, still flexes. Most wearable shoe models (like the Airberry) are tuned for TPU 90A.

95A+ outsole only

Great where the shoe meets concrete — hard-wearing tread that survives the street. But a WHOLE shoe in 95A? Heavy, stiff, zero bounce. That's the trap.

MOR
PHLEX
The cheat code: BIQU MorPhlex. A "variable hardness" TPU made for shoes. It prints firm at 90A — so it feeds through the printer like a normal, easy filament — then softens to about 75A afterwards. You print at brick-hardness, you walk on cloud-softness. Best of both worlds, and exactly why soft-after, firm-during beats plain hard TPU.

03Print Rules

TPU isn't hard — it's just different. Six rules and your shoes come out right:

  1. Go slow. 20–40 mm/s (20–30 for extra-soft). Almost every failed flexible print is speed, not the material.
  2. Direct drive only for soft TPU. Pushing floppy filament through a long Bowden tube is pushing a wet noodle — it buckles. 95A survives Bowden; 85A and softer usually won't.
  3. Dry the filament. TPU drinks moisture from the air → stringy, bubbly, weak prints. Dry it before a big shoe print.
  4. Print them standing. Shoes print upright on the plate — that's how the designs are meant to go, and standing on textured PEI they need no supports (TPU supports fuse on and never come off).
  5. Use the designer's infill. If the model page or its print profile says a specific infill — pattern or percentage — use exactly that: the designer tuned it for the shoe. No spec given? Then 10–25% gyroid is your fallback — it flexes evenly like foam. More air = squishier step.
  6. Wear them wet. TPU is abrasion-tough and doesn't care about water — people print shower slides. Mild soap, rinse, air-dry.
Fit tip: print ONE insole first. One flat insole tests your size, your filament and your printer for pennies — before you commit 40 hours to a full pair.
