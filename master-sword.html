▲ The One Rule ▲
The secret isn't the printing — it's the sanding.
Prime, sand, prime, sand… until every layer line disappears. Patience is what turns a print into a forged blade.

Cheap “from China” PLA is totally fine for a sand-and-paint prop — just pick a semi-decent budget brand (eSun, Sunlu…) so you're not fighting jams.

Finish It

The printing is the easy part. This is where a good sword is actually made — budget a few evenings.

  1. Join the segments — a dab of superglue or epoxy at each seam. (Popandsicle's design even snaps together, so you may barely need glue.)
  2. Fill the seams — Bondo spot putty into every joint line.
  3. Sand — 120 → 220 → 400 grit, knocking down the layer lines.
  4. Filler primer — a heavy coat that fills the fine texture.
  5. Sand & repeat — prime, sand, prime again until the lines vanish — then wet-sand fine: 600 → 800 → 1000 grit for a glass-smooth, forged-metal blade (the finish the Vader manual swears by). This is the secret; patience beats skill.
  6. Paint — mask and lay down the colours below.
  7. Clear coat — matte on the grip, a little gloss on the blade.

Paint It

Blade — silver / steel (a whisper of blue) Winged guard & grip — blue Fittings, wrap & Triforce — gold Centre gem — amber (or just paint it blue) Optional — the sacred blue glow (LED / backlit)

Only got silver, blue, and a gold spray? That's basically the whole sword — silver blade, blue hilt, gold accents. You're already set.

Through the Years

The Blade of Evil's Bane has been reforged in every era. Here's how it changed — pick the version you want to build.

1991
A Link to the Past — its debut. Purple grip & blue guard in the pedestal, red grip & gold guard once drawn — and that crystal-blue blade that's been its signature ever since.
1998
Ocarina of Time — the version everyone pictures. Blue handle, green grip wrap, the blue winged guard, a yellow gem, and the Triforce etched on the blade. First time in full 3D.
2002
The Wind Waker — toon-shaded and chunky, a bold purple grip. Lost its power… then woke back up.
2006
Twilight Princess — darker, grittier and battle-ready. A deep blue-purple grip and a heavier, realistic look — the "cool" one.
2011
Skyward Sword — the origin. It starts as the Goddess Sword (with the spirit Fi living inside it) and gets tempered, step by step, into the Master Sword itself.
2013
A Link Between Worlds — back to the classic top-down look, and temperable into stronger, golden forms.
2017
Breath of the Wild — ancient and battle-worn, glowing crystalline blue near Malice. The one most people 3D-print.
2023
Tears of the Kingdom — decayed by Gloom and shattered, then restored longer and reforged. Fuse it with items to change its look and power.

Before You Start

⚠ Stay safe, Hero
  • ◆ Wear a dust mask when sanding — PLA dust is fine and you don't want it in your lungs.
  • ◆ Prime and spray outside or by an open window. Primer and paint fumes need fresh air.
  • ◆ Get a grown-up for the sharp + hot stuff — craft knives, files, the printer nozzle and a soldering iron for the optional LED all bite.
  • ◆ This is a display prop. Leave the edge blunt, don't swing it at anyone, and remember PLA is brittle — it's for photos and shelves, not battles.
